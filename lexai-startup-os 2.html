Founder Survival Guide
The 10 Mistakes That
Kill Startups
"Most startups don't fail because of competition or bad luck. They fail because the founder stopped talking to users, built the wrong thing, or ran out of energy. Avoid these mistakes and you are in the top 10%."
Fatal Mistake #1
Building Without Talking to Users
You lock yourself in a room for 3 months and build. You launch. Nobody uses it. The fix: talk to 1 user every single day. Even a quick 5-minute DM counts. Build what they ask for, not what you imagine they want.
Fatal Mistake #2
Waiting Until It's "Ready" to Launch
There is no ready. Launch when it works for 1 person. Paul Graham's rule: "If you're not embarrassed by your first version, you launched too late." Launch ugly. Fix in public. Speed beats perfection every time.
Fatal Mistake #3
Spending Money Before Revenue
Do not spend $5,000 on a logo, $3,000 on a website designer, or $10,000 on ads before you have 10 paying customers. Every dollar you spend before product-market fit is a dollar you'll regret. Bootstrap hard. Your total cost should be under $200/month until $1K MRR.
Fatal Mistake #4
Building Too Many Features
You want to build 20 features. Build 3. Pick the ones that directly reduce the #1 pain your users described. Everything else goes to a "Future" list. Every feature you add before launch is a feature that delayed your launch by a week.
Fatal Mistake #5
Giving Up After 3 Months
Most startups that succeed took 12–24 months to find product-market fit. The overnight successes took years. Set a minimum commitment: "I will work on this for 12 months before considering quitting." Show up every day. Progress is invisible until it isn't.
Fatal Mistake #6
Pricing Too Low
Founders are scared of charging money. They price at $9/month. At $9/month you need 11,000 customers for $100K ARR. At $99/month you need 1,000. At $299/month you need 333. Charge more. Customers who pay more are better customers. They use the product, give feedback, and stay longer.
Fatal Mistake #7
No Marketing for 6 Months
You think "I'll market when the product is done." The product is never done. Start your LinkedIn content in Week 1 — before you write a single line of code. Build in public from Day 1. By the time you launch, you should have a warm audience of 500+ people who know who you are.
Fatal Mistake #8
Ignoring Churn
10 users sign up. 6 cancel after 30 days. Most founders ignore the 6 and focus on getting 10 more. This is wrong. Interview every single person who cancels. The reason they leave is your most valuable business intelligence. Fix that reason before acquiring new users.
Fatal Mistake #9
Raising Money Too Early
Raising $500K before you have 10 customers seems great. It's often fatal. You hire people before you understand the business. You spend on marketing before you know what channel works. Money amplifies both good and bad. Get to $1K MRR first. Then raise.
Fatal Mistake #10
Working Alone in Silence
Join YC's Startup School (free). Join founder communities on Slack (OnDeck, South Park Commons). Find 3 other solo founders and do a weekly check-in call. Your mental health matters. Isolation kills momentum. Share your wins and losses publicly — it attracts customers and investors.
